# Architecture & Project Decisions

## ADR-001: Hybrid Translation Strategy (Option 2)
- **Context:** Translating ~600k–700k English words across 34 chapters (765 tips) from scratch requires significant API spend and execution time.
- **Decision:** Ingest the CC BY 4.0 English dataset from `parveen0029/The-Evidence-Based-Life` containing all 765 tips matching the chapter structure. Apply normalization, glossary consistency checks, and a humanizing refinement pass to fix stripped citations and enforce direct second-person style.
- **Consequences:** Saves 8–12 hours of API run time and eliminates unnecessary LLM translation cost while retaining strict alignment with the Chinese source.

## ADR-002: Humanizer Integration
- **Context:** Machine/LLM-generated translations frequently introduce AI tells (staged run-ups, forced triads, dramatic one-line closers).
- **Decision:** Install `blader/humanizer` into `.agents/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md`. Enforce that humanizer edits apply strictly to prose rhythm and natural phrasing, leaving numbers, percentages, units, currency (¥ RMB), DOIs, and URLs unchanged.
- **Consequences:** Consistent, natural voice across tips without violating faithful translation non-negotiables.

## ADR-003: Typeface Roles
- **Context:** The site requires a distinct reading serif, a clean UI sans, and a comic display font.
- **Decision:** Self-host:
  - Reading serif: **Literata** (body 17–18px, line-height 1.7, 60–68 char measure).
  - UI sans: **Bricolage Grotesque** (navigation, chips, metadata).
  - Comic display: **Lilita One** (<25KB, Latin subset) strictly for SFX stickers, speech bubbles, splash titles, and Home headline.
- **Consequences:** Zero external font dependencies, no layout shift (`font-display: swap`), strict typography discipline.

## ADR-004: Liquid Glass vs. Fake Glass Performance Boundary
- **Context:** Applying `backdrop-filter: blur(24px)` across 765 scrolling tip cards causes severe GPU lag and breaks 60fps mobile budgets.
- **Decision:** Real Liquid Glass (`backdrop-filter: blur(24px) saturate(180%) brightness(1.06)`) is restricted to floating nav bar, sheets, and modal dialogs (maximum 3 active surfaces). Tip cards use opaque "fake glass" backgrounds with static lighting highlights to preserve 60fps and guarantee $\ge 7:1$ text contrast.
- **Consequences:** Flawless mobile scroll performance with near-zero CLS.

## ADR-005: Initial Calm Mode Chapters
- **Context:** The comic cast and particle celebrations must not appear on chapters covering heavy topics.
- **Decision:** Chapters 1, 13, 16, 25, 28, 29, 30, and 33 run in Calm Mode (`data/calm-chapters.json`), disabling Plus & Minus, SFX stickers, and particle bursts in favor of a quiet glow.
- **Consequences:** Respectful reader experience on sensitive topics.

## ADR-006: Batched Progressive Feed Rendering & Touch Target Expansion
- **Context:** Rendering 765 recommendation cards synchronously in `explore.astro` created over 23,000 DOM nodes, causing stutter on mobile typing and filter switching. Furthermore, dense elements like the 14px beads in `ReadingMap.astro` were difficult to tap accurately on small mobile touchscreens.
- **Decision:**
  - Implement batched progressive display in `explore.astro` using an initial 25-card slice and an `IntersectionObserver` sentinel that progressively unhides subsequent batches on scroll, paired with 120ms debounce and `requestAnimationFrame`.
  - Use `::after` absolute pseudo-elements (`44px × 44px`) to expand interactive hit areas without altering the compact visual aesthetic of the bead field.
- **Consequences:** 60fps filter transitions, zero keyboard lag, and full WCAG 2.5.5 / Section 10 touch compliance across all mobile viewports down to 360px.

## ADR-007: Material Ladder Replaces the Fake-Glass Card
- **Context:** ADR-004 kept real `backdrop-filter` on chrome and used `.fake-glass` for the 765 tip cards. In practice `.fake-glass` was still a lit surface — jade-tinted border, a white top bevel and a drop shadow — so every tip read as a floating glass tile, and the card carried six nested rounded boxes (badges, chips, two metric blocks, a caveats panel, a sources drawer) inside a seventh.
- **Decision:** Introduce a two-step material ladder in `src/styles/ui.css`: `.paper` (hairline border, card background, no shadow) for content surfaces and `.plate` (same plus a 2px jade top edge) for one emphasised panel per page. Tip cards, pagination cards and the home panels move to `.paper`; the tip card overrides its background to `--theme-card-strong` so body copy keeps its 7:1 margin. `backdrop-filter` is now chrome only: sticky chapter header, bottom nav, settings sheet, reading map.
- **Consequences:** Fewer compositor layers while scrolling, one consistent border vocabulary across the site, and content surfaces that read as pages rather than tiles. Contrast is unchanged because the background is opaque and the text tokens did not move. ADR-004's performance boundary still holds; its material choice for content surfaces is superseded by this entry.

## ADR-008: Type Roles Are Serif for Reading, Sans for Labels
- **Context:** Measured from the shipped `fvar` tables, `literata-regular.woff2` carries `opsz 7–72, wght 400–900` and `bricolage-grotesque.woff2` carries `opsz 12–96, wght 200–800`, but `@font-face` declared only `400` for Literata and `400 700` for Bricolage. Every `font-weight: 800` was therefore clamped to 700, and bold Literata in body copy was browser-synthesised.
- **Decision:** Declare the real ranges in `src/styles/fonts.css`. Give headline duty to the reading serif — chapter titles and all 765 tip titles are Literata 600 — and keep Bricolage for labels, chrome, metadata and numerals. No font file is added, so the 221,088 B payload and the privacy rule are untouched.
- **Consequences:** Headlines are heavier than before (real 600, not synthesised), and the reading voice and the headline voice are the same face. Labels stay sans and tracked, which keeps the two roles legible at a glance. The italic file still has no `wght` axis; declared as 400 only.

## ADR-009: View Transitions Are Cut Into Named Groups
- **Context:** Route transitions were defined twice — once in a scoped `<style>` block in `BaseLayout.astro`, where they could never match, and once globally in `comic.css`. The live copy animated the root group with a translate and a rotation, which moved the left rail, the bottom nav and the sticky chapter header along with the page.
- **Decision:** Name three groups in `comic.css`: `chrome-rail`, `chrome-nav`, `reading-column`. Root and chrome hold still; the reading column leaves in 130 ms and arrives in 260 ms, transform and opacity only. Reduced motion collapses the duration to 1 ms. The dead scoped block is deleted rather than repaired.
- **Consequences:** Navigation now reads as the page changing rather than the window lurching, and the animation is compositor-only. Acceptance Check 9 ("panel wipe or fade fallback, no layout shift") is satisfied by the fade fallback; no scroll-linked or JS-driven motion was added.

## ADR-010: Monsoon Glass — Thick Glass Reading Cards, Gated Blur, Folded Evidence
- **Context:** The reader asked for a full-glass reading surface as a hard requirement and, separately, that each tip's Benefits/Caveats block stop dominating the page. ADR-004 banned glass on content surfaces for performance and honesty reasons, and ADR-007 replaced the fake-glass card with an opaque `.paper` page. Both constraints are still real: 47 cards on a chapter page with a live `backdrop-filter` each cannot hold 60 fps on a throttled phone.
- **Decision:** Keep glass on content by making it thick rather than thin. Tip cards use `--glass-thick`: a 72% tint of the page ground, a hairline, and a lit top edge; only cards currently in view get `backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(118%)`, added by an IntersectionObserver capped at 3 cards (`src/scripts/glass-cards.ts`), and only when the reader has not asked for reduced transparency. The moving element is the ground (`src/styles/ground.css`), never the type. Offscreen cards declare `content-visibility: auto; contain-intrinsic-size: auto 420px` so they cost no layout. Cost, Benefit and Caveats move into a fold whose collapsed state is the default, so a chapter now reads as titles and plain words with the evidence one tap away.
- **Consequences:** The reading surface is genuinely glass: colour moves behind the text, at 15.98–18.35:1 measured contrast because the 72% tint is near-opaque and the ground's accents sit at 11–16% alpha. The blur budget is bounded by construction rather than by discipline. Mobile Lighthouse moved from 88 to 91 performance (FCP 2.6 s, LCP 2.8 s, TBT 0 ms, CLS 0.041) and 100 accessibility, and the fold removes roughly 860 px of evidence per tip from the default page. Costs accepted: font payload grows by 63,108 B for Mukta (net −13,780 B after removing Bricolage, which never had the rupee glyph it was kept for), and the fold needs JavaScript for its animation while staying readable without it. ADR-004's "no blur on content" boundary is superseded for in-view cards only; ADR-007's opaque `.paper` reading card is superseded by glass; ADR-008's Bricolage role as interface face is superseded by Mukta, with Literata still carrying the reading column.

## ADR-011: Measure Before You Restyle; Review Screens Through a Vision Model

- **Context:** The session model cannot accept image input, so "does the interface look right" could not be answered by looking. The first review pass used a vision model (modlens CLI) and Orca's browser to read screenshots, and it returned usable defects - a contradictory pair of status chips, a 30 px content box inside a 23 px pill, an orphaned "/" starting a wrapped metadata line, body text showing through the fixed bottom nav - alongside claims that did not survive checking: a "contrast failure" on the teal ROI pill and on the uppercase metadata labels, and an "empty right column" on the home page.
- **Decision:**
  1. Treat a vision model as a defect *finder*, not a defect *judge*. Every claim is re-measured in the browser (bounding boxes, scrollHeight vs clientHeight, computed colours composited over the actual painted background, or a modal colour sampled from a screenshot crop) before a value is changed.
  2. Fix contrast only when the arithmetic fails. The flagged pill measured 7.06 / 9.08 / 5.15 / 5.19 : 1 across Neel / Monsoon / Chandni / Kora and the labels 9.95 / 9.18 / 7.60 / 7.06 : 1 - all above the 4.5:1 AA floor, so nothing was darkened; a passing accent was left alone, because darkening it would have cost the palette its one lively colour.
  3. Keep the small geometry of dense metadata in tokens on the card (.tip-card sets --badge-fs, --badge-pad, --badge-h, --chip-sep), because a component's scoped rule compiles to .class[data-astro-cid-hash] at specificity 0,2,0 while an @media block is emitted unscoped at 0,1,0 and would otherwise lose to it.
  4. Inside a card, one divider vocabulary: the fold's brass rule is the only deliberate horizontal break; disclosures (sources, actions) are quiet rows rather than filled rounded boxes nested in the card's own border.
  5. Decline findings that are the content doing its job. US/NHTSA figures under an "India edition" banner are faithful translation (data/en-in/chapters/01-do-not-die-early.json), and the home column's breathing room is the centred shell (rail 240 px + main 760 px in a 1440 px viewport).
- **Consequences:** The visual pass produced seven real fixes and dropped three plausible-sounding ones, each with the number that justified the call. A broken CSS comment was found in the process: the @media (max-width: 639px) block in src/components/TipCard.astro had been silently dead, so its mobile rules never shipped. The remaining accepted compromise is documented rather than hidden: a fixed nav capsule still passes over body text mid-scroll, mitigated by hide-on-scroll, a 104 px scrim and 6.25 rem of reserved shell height.

## ADR-012: Brass Is a Material, Progress Is a Contour

- **Context:** The reader asked for a monsoon ornament language — a kolam reading map, an arch over the daily panel, a seal at chapter end, a ruled rupee reference — on a site whose rules forbid decoration that touches the advice, layout that moves, and characters inside the reading column. The map is 765 beads in 34 cluster grids; the ornament had to read at 360 px and survive with JavaScript blocked.
- **Decision:**
  1. **Brass is a material, not a second palette.** `--color-brass` stays defined exactly once (`src/styles/tokens.css:11`) and ornament reaches it only through mixes in `src/styles/ornament.css`: `--orn-line` 62% / 82%, raised to 72% / 92% on light palettes. The ladder is thickness, not hue — hairline 1px, rule 2px, bead 5px — so an ornament cannot become a colour accent and no token shadows a shipped one.
  2. **Progress is one contour, not 765 decorations.** Each cluster gets a single closed petal-cusp path in a 100x100 box, stretched with `preserveAspectRatio="none"` and held at one device pixel by `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`; `pathLength="1"` lets `stroke-dashoffset: calc(1 - var(--kolam-trace, 0))` read as a fraction. The map script writes one custom property per cluster from the ledger it already subscribes to. A serpentine that threaded the beads was rejected: `repeat(auto-fill, 20px)` reflows at every width, so it would need three media-queried SVGs and every one of 765 anchor positions recalculated, and the cusps would shear between breakpoints.
  3. **The lattice is drawn twice on purpose.** The dim base path is the lattice itself — present before anything is read and with scripting blocked — and the lit loop on top is the only progress-dependent stroke. Without the base, a fresh or JS-blocked map showed no contour at all, which the JS-blocked contract forbids.
  4. **The arch sits above the panel, never over it.** A `clip-path` intrados would clip the title link's focus ring and distort at 360 px, so the crown is a separate 22 px stroke-only SVG above the panel's existing top rule; the panel keeps its radius, padding, `overflow: hidden` and backdrop-filter. Measured at 360 px with 130% text: 17 px side clearance and 48 px above the title link, against a 5 px focus-ring extent (2 px outline + 3 px offset).
  5. **The seal outlives Calm Mode and Comic effects off.** It is static markup, `aria-hidden`, stroke only; the settle is one opacity and one ring dashoffset (380 ms, 120 ms delay) and only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. Calm chapters keep the panel and lose the ink frame and the cast, so completion reads as a page closing rather than a curtain call.
  6. **Overlapping 44 px targets resolve to the nearest bead.** Measured on the shipped map: the 44 px hit areas sit on a 20 px lattice, so the browser gives the press to whichever painted last — pressing tip 1-3 landed on 1-13. Shrinking the targets would break Check 5 and widening the lattice would redraw the map, so a pointer press is resolved geometrically to the nearest bead centre; keyboard activation (detail 0) and non-primary buttons keep the anchor's own href.
- **Consequences:** The ornament cap became measurable rather than aspirational — home 1 arch / 0 kolam / 0 seal, chapter 0 / 0 / 1, `/progress/` 34 contours, no motif inside a tip card — and `tests/ornament.spec.ts` enforces it across dark/light x reduced motion on/off x Comic effects on/off. Rendered reader text stayed byte-identical to the pre-change build, and reader JS grew 0.3 KB gzip on `/progress/` only. Accepted cost: the kolam contour is decorative rather than a second data mark; the beads still carry read state and the details table stays the accessible mirror. The calm-chapter frame change also fixed a dead guard in `src/cast/particle-burst.ts`, which had been reading `document.body` for a flag that lives on `.chapter-page-root`.
