## How to read this pack

Each row records one high-risk legal or financial claim, the official instrument that
governs it, the section or page, the access date and a verdict:

- **supported** - accurate under the law in force on the access date.
- **superseded** - an accurate statement of a statute that has since been repealed or
  replaced, and that the tip still presents as current law.
- **unsupported** - no credible official source was found, or the source contradicts
  the claim.

### Decisive finding

The four labour codes were brought into force by four Ministry of Labour and Employment
notifications dated 21 November 2025:

- S.O. 5322(E) - Code on Wages, 2019 (sections 1-41, 42(4)-(9), 43-66, 67, 68, 69).
- S.O. 5321(E) - Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (all provisions).
- S.O. 5320(E) - Industrial Relations Code, 2020 (all provisions).
- S.O. 5319(E) - Code on Social Security, 2020 (sections 1-14, 15(1)-(2), 16(1)(c), 17-141,
  143, 144-163, and Items 1, 2 and 4-9 of section 164(1)).

Consequences that decide most rows below:

1. The Factories Act, 1948 is repealed by the OSH Code (one of 13 repealed enactments).
2. The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 is repealed by section 104(1) of the Industrial
   Relations Code; the Industrial Relations Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 (assent
   16 February 2026, retrospective to 21 November 2025) rewrote section 104(1) and
   confirmed the repeal of the Trade Unions Act, 1926, the Standing Orders Act, 1946 and
   the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
3. The Employees Compensation Act, 1923, the ESIC Act, 1948, the Payment of Gratuity Act,
   1972 and the Contract Labour Act, 1970 are repealed (items 1, 2 and 6 of section
   164(1) of the Code on Social Security; Contract Labour by the OSH Code).
4. The Employees Provident Funds Act, 1952 is **not** repealed: item 3 of section 164(1)
   of the Code on Social Security was deliberately left out of S.O. 5319(E), so the 1952
   Act continues in force.

## No-equivalent notes

- Tip 19-1: China's 1.5x / 2x / 3x overtime ladder and the 50%-100% additional penalty have
  no Indian equivalent. The closest Indian route is the double-rate overtime provision
  (Factories Act s.59, now OSH Code) plus a labour-authority complaint.
- Tip 19-2: China's 5/10/15-day ladder by total years of service and 300% unused-leave
  payment have no Indian equivalent. The Indian route is one day per 20 days worked.
- Tip 19-3: China's 1/2/6-month probation caps, single-probation rule and 80% floor have no
  Indian statutory equivalent; probation is contractual and, for covered establishments,
  governed by standing orders.
- Tip 19-4: China's N (one month per year, capped at three times average wage and 12 years)
  has no Indian equivalent. India uses 15 days' average pay per completed year.
- Tip 19-5: China's N+1 has no Indian equivalent; notice is contractual or statutory.
- Tip 19-6: China's 2N has no Indian equivalent; Indian remedies are discretionary.
- Tip 19-18: China's three-times-local-average-wage exemption has no Indian equivalent;
  India uses the s.10(10B) limit.
- Tip 19-20: China's double-wage penalty for a missing written contract has no Indian
  equivalent.

## Open issues

1. **BLOCKING - repealed statutes presented as current law.** Tips 19-1, 19-2, 19-4, 19-5,
   19-6, 19-7, 19-11, 19-13, 19-14, 19-15, 19-24 (and the citations in 19-3, 19-9, 19-20,
   19-23) rely on the Factories Act, 1948, the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947, the Employees
   Compensation Act, 1923, the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 and the Contract Labour Act,
   1970. All were repealed with effect from 21 November 2025.
2. **BLOCKING - the nine-hour day is wrong.** Tip 19-1 states a 9-hour daily cap. The OSH
   Code, s.25(1)(a) fixes eight hours a day. Only the 48-hour week survives.
3. **BLOCKING - leave arithmetic.** Tip 19-2 says 'roughly 15 days a year'. At the statutory
   240-day threshold (now 180 days under OSH Code s.32) the entitlement is 12 days (9 under
   the Code); 15 days requires about 300 days worked. The tip does not say this.
4. **BLOCKING - unsupported safety-retaliation claim.** Tip 19-10 asserts that retaliation
   for a safety complaint 'is not lawful' and cites nothing that says so.
5. **BLOCKING - commuting injury.** Tip 19-11 says commuting injuries 'can be covered in
   some ESIC cases'. ESIC employment injury requires an accident arising out of and in the
   course of employment; the commute is generally outside it. No source is given.
6. **BLOCKING - emergency numbers.** Tip 19-12 cites 112.gov.in, which did not respond on
   the access date, and asserts 108 'in most states' with no source.
7. **Dead citation.** Tip 19-16 cites wcd.nic.in, which no longer resolves; the POSH Act
   page is now on wcd.gov.in. The substantive POSH claim holds.
8. **Imprecise drafting.** Tip 19-14 says compensation is 'not a fixed grade table', but the
   EC Act's Schedule I fixed percentages for specified injuries (now carried into the SS Code).
9. **Thin citations.** Several tips cite only the labour.gov.in home page for a specific
   statute. A home page cannot support a section-level legal claim.
10. **Non-blocking.** Tip 19-24's coverage statement should note the OSH Code threshold of 50
    contract workers (s.45), up from 20 under the repealed Act.
