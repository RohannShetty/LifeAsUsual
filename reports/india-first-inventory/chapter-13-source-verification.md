# Chapter 13 source verification — emergencies and first aid

Checked 20 November 2026. All-India scope unless a state is named. This is an automated editorial source audit: it records what official pages do and do not support. It is not professional medical review.

## Why this chapter was audited first

Chapter 13 gives instructions that a reader may follow in the first minutes of an emergency. A wrong instruction here can hurt someone, so the four safety-critical claims below were corrected before any wider cleanup.

## Wrong instructions corrected

| Tip | What it said | Why it was wrong | What it says now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 13-15 anaphylaxis | "Call 112 … so emergency responders can bring epinephrine with them" | No Indian emergency-service page found that documents 112 or 108 ambulances carrying epinephrine. Promising the drug could make a reader wait instead of using a prescribed auto-injector. | Call 112, state "suspected anaphylactic shock", do not count on responders carrying epinephrine, use a prescribed auto-injector if available. |
| 13-23 heatstroke | Benefit text quoted "Do not give the patient any water to drink" | Contradicts NDMA heat-wave guidance, which tells responders to give a conscious person ORS, lemon sarbat or salted water. The quoted line came from a different context and read as a blanket ban. | Cool first; if fully conscious, give ORS, lemon sarbat or lightly salted water in small sips; nothing by mouth if unconscious or confused. |
| 13-30 earthquake | "If it's an old structure prone to collapse, head outside right away" | Contradicts NDMA, which says to stay inside until the shaking stops because most injuries happen to people moving or trying to leave during the shaking. | Stay inside until the shaking stops, drop, cover, hold on; move to open ground only after the shaking ends. The Armenian evacuation study is kept, with its limits stated. |
| 13-3 stroke | "108 is the state ambulance service in most states" | The 108 service and its number differ by state and union territory; the claim was not sourced. | 112 is the all-India ERSS number; 108 operates in many but not all states, so remember 112. |

## Invented prices removed

13-9 (head CT "a few hundred rupees"), 13-10 (Rs 2,800-4,300) and 13-11 (Rs 1,400-2,800) carried rupee figures with no Indian source. Hospital charges vary by state, hospital type and city, so all three now say the rate varies and the hospital should be asked, without implying a national price.

## Added

- 13-20 poisoning: the National Poisons Information Centre at AIIMS runs a 24x7 toll-free helpline, 1800 116 117. Verified on the official AIIMS page (https://aiims.edu/index.php?id=167&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article), which states "Toll Free No. - 1800 116 117".

## Sources that could not be read

- 112.gov.in: TLS certificate is self-signed; the page could not be read. The claim about 112 is therefore cited to the MHA ERSS page instead.
- ndma.gov.in deep links under /Natural-Hazards/ return the site's "page not found" shell; NDMA guidance is cited at the site root, and its wording is summarised rather than quoted where only the root page could be loaded.
- ndma.gov.in fire and lightning pages are image-only, so no text could be matched.
- Many first-aid techniques in this chapter trace to AHA, ILCOR, CDC, NHS, AAOS and IADT guidance, which is international. Those techniques are not Indian-specific and are not presented as such.
