## Blocking issues

- **5-7** — Presents China's PBOC credit-card rule as Indian law: "Before 2020, credit-card interest rates were capped: the maximum daily rate was 0.05% and the minimum was 70% of that cap ... Starting in 2021, issuers and cardholders can negotiate rates freely." India has had no such cap since RBI deregulation in 2006. **Correction:** replace the paragraph with the Indian position — card interest is set by the issuer and disclosed in the most-important-terms-and-conditions; RBI requires transparency, not a cap; the minimum-due trap is the real point.
- **5-26** — States that third-party property damage is "capped, at ₹7,500", and the caveat says "the one number in the Act is the property-damage limit". After the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019, s.147(2) no longer fixes that amount (it provides for a Central Government-prescribed base premium); the standard TPPD cover is ₹7.5 lakh for private cars and ₹1 lakh for two-wheelers under the India Motor Tariff. **Correction:** remove the ₹7,500 figure and the "one number in the Act" sentence; say the Act leaves the amount to the prescribed terms and that TPPD cover is ₹7.5 lakh (car) / ₹1 lakh (two-wheeler).
- **5-30** — Imports China's 2024 Consumer Protection Regulation: the platform is said to be "legally obligated" to furnish the livestream operator's and influencer's details, and the caveat describes a statutory seven-day no-questions-asked return whose exclusions merchants cannot expand, must not default to, and cannot deny without consent. No Indian instrument creates these duties, and the claim contradicts 5-23. **Correction:** rewrite as China-only (or delete the legal-obligation claim); for India, rely on the E-Commerce Rules 2020 seller-identity/grievance-officer duties and the CPA 2019, and keep 5-23's statement that there is no general statutory seven-day return.
