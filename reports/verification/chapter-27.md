## Method

I compared the India build (data/en-in/chapters/27-pregnancy-and-childbirth.json, 20 tips) against the source chapter in data/en/all-chapters.json, then checked each Indian legal, procedural, financial and medical claim against an instrument or paper I actually fetched on 2026-10-21. Indian instruments were pulled through the India Code DSpace API (https://indiacode.gov.in/server/api/...) and its bitstreams, and programme claims from PIB releases (pib.gov.in), the National Health Mission site (nhm.gov.in), ESIC (esic.gov.in) and MoHFW. Clinical claims were checked against PubMed abstracts (pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), WHO (who.int, iris.who.int) and USPSTF (uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org). Every DOI in the chapter was resolved through https://api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>.

Access failures, recorded rather than papered over: www.indiacode.nic.in returns HTTP 403/504 (the readable host is https://indiacode.gov.in); naco.gov.in does not resolve (curl exit 000), so the NACO/NVHCP pages behind tip 27-3 could not be read; nha.gov.in serves a client-rendered JavaScript shell, so the PM-JAY pages behind tips 27-11 and 27-14 could not be read; cdc.gov rejects curl with HTTP 403 (the same page loads through web_fetch and was read that way). Where a page could not be read I marked the claim Partly or Unverified and did not treat the URL as proof.

Banned-term check: `npx tsx scripts/check-chapter.ts 27` -> PASS: no banned term, CJK character or foreign-jurisdiction term (20 tips). The JSON and the rendered page were both checked.

## Safety and editorial check

- No unsupported high-risk claim: the emergency, medication and vaccine tips (27-1, 27-5, 27-8, 27-9, 27-10, 27-18) all match a fetched authoritative source, dose for dose and grade for grade.
- No invented scheme, number or hotline: PMSMA's 9th-of-the-month, JSY's 12 April 2005 and Rs 1,400/Rs 600, PMMVY's Rs 5,000/Rs 6,000, ESIC's 26 weeks and Rs 15,000, HBNC's six visits by day 42, and the 112/108 and 14416 numbers all check out.
- No direct equivalent recorded where needed: the chapter says explicitly that India has no universal newborn screening programme (27-13) and no single maternity insurance (27-11), rather than inventing one.
- Caveats retained: the chapter keeps the "no national price" caveat for epidurals and the state-variation caveats for ambulance and screening. The attribution of the 21-day rule to the 1969 Act rather than its Rules is the one place where the caveat is thinner than the source.
