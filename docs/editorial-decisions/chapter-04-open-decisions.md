# Chapter 4 — open editorial decisions

Status: **EDITORIAL RECOMMENDATIONS REVISED, NONE APPROVED.** This revision corrects
two factual errors in the 19 November packet (D4 and D5) after checking the official
MoSPI Time Use Survey reports. No row below has an owner decision. The shipped chapter
is unchanged.

## What this revision corrects

- **D5 was based on a false premise.** The earlier packet said the rewrite correctly
  states that "India publishes nothing comparable" to a national time-use survey. That is
  wrong: MoSPI/NSO ran all-India Time Use Surveys in 2019 and again in January–December
  2024, and published *Time Use in India, 2024* on 28 March 2025. The recommendation is
  rewritten to cite that survey and to remove the false claim.
- **D4 proposed an unsourced universal quantity** ("a household that outsources nothing
  loses an hour or more a day"). No source supports that sentence for Indian households.
  The recommendation now uses the TUS 2024 figures that do exist, and drops the universal
  claim.
- D1, D2, D3, D6 and D7 keep their earlier reasoning, with the evidence locators made
  explicit so a reviewer can check each one.

## Evidence appendix

Official sources checked on 19 November 2026:

- MoSPI, *Time Use in India, 2024 (January–December 2024)*, report released 28 March 2025:
  https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/TUS_Report_2024_28.03.2025F.pdf
- PIB release, "REPORT: TIME USE IN INDIA, 2024", 28 March 2025:
  https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2116301
- PIB press note, "Time Use Survey (January–December 2024)", 25 February 2025:
  https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2106113

Quoted findings used above (release text):

- "Female participants aged 15-59 years in unpaid domestic services spent about 315
  minutes during 2019 in those activities, which has come down to 305 minutes during
  2024."
- "female participants in caregiving activities spent about 140 minutes in a day,
  compared to 74 minutes spent by male participants aged 15-59 years."
- "People aged 6 years and above spent 11 per cent of their days' time in culture,
  leisure, mass media and sports practices during 2024, compared to 9.9 per cent of
  the days' time spent during 2019."
